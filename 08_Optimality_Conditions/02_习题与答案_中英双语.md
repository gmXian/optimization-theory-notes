## Exercise 8.1

**考点：无约束极值的一、二阶条件。** 令 $\phi(t)=f(x^*+tv)$。若 $x^*$ 是局部极小点，则对任意 $v$，$t=0$ 是 $\phi$ 的局部极小点，所以

$$\phi'(0)=\nabla f(x^*)^\top v=0\quad\forall v,$$

故 $\boxed{\nabla f(x^*)=0}$。又有 $\phi''(0)=v^\top H(x^*)v\ge0$，所以 $H(x^*)\succeq0$。反之若 $\nabla f(x^*)=0$ 且 $H(x^*)\succ0$，Taylor 展开给

$$f(x^*+h)-f(x^*)=\frac12h^\top H(x^*)h+o(\|h\|^2)>0,$$

因此 $x^*$ 为严格局部极小点。对 $-f$ 应用同一结论即得到极大值版 Theorem 2。

**English conclusion.** A local minimum requires zero gradient and a positive-semidefinite Hessian; positive definiteness is sufficient for a strict local minimum.

## Exercise 8.3

令 $\phi(t)=f(x^*+tv)$。若 $x^*$ 是相对极大点且充分小的负 $t$ 保持可行，则单侧必要条件给 $\nabla f(x^*)^\top v\le0$；若一阶项为零，则 $v^\top H(x^*)v\le0$。相对极小点时不等号反向。

当 $S=\mathbb R_+^n$ 且 $x^*$ 是相对极小点：若 $x_i^*>0$ 可沿 $\pm e_i$ 运动，故 $\partial_i f=0$；若 $x_i^*=0$ 只能向正方向，故 $\partial_i f\ge0$。因此
$$\boxed{\nabla f(x^*)\ge0,\qquad \nabla f(x^*)^\top x^*=0,\qquad x^*\ge0.}$$

## Exercise 8.11

令最优值函数 $V(b)=f(x^*(b))$。链式法则与 stationarity 给
$$\frac{\partial V}{\partial b_i}=\nabla f(x^*)^\top\frac{\partial x^*}{\partial b_i}=\lambda^{*\top}g_x(x^*)\frac{\partial x^*}{\partial b_i}.$$
对 $g(x^*(b))=b$ 求导得 $g_x(x^*)\frac{\partial x^*}{\partial b_i}=e_i$，所以
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial V}{\partial b_i}=\lambda_i^*.}$$
即 Lagrange multiplier 是 RHS 的 shadow value。

## Exercise 8.18

若 $(x^*,\lambda^*)$ 是 saddle point，固定 $\lambda^*$，$x^*$ 在非负正交象限最大化 $L$，故
$$L_x\le0,\quad x^*\ge0,\quad L_x^\top x^*=0.$$

$$L_\lambda=b-g(x^*)\ge0,\quad\lambda^*\ge0,\quad L_\lambda^\top\lambda^*=0.$$

Exercise 8.21(d) 的最小化二次规划可写
$$u=c+Bx+A^\top\lambda\ge0,\qquad v=b-Ax\ge0,$$

## Exercise 8.23 — 四个 quadratic programming 数值题

教材 Problem (34) 为

$$
\max
displaystyle\quad
c^Tx+\frac12x^TBx
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
Ax\le b,
\qquad x\ge0.
$$

本题扫描页经视觉复核后，四组矩阵可以无歧义恢复；此前“矩阵不可读”的旧说明已删除。

### (a)

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
A=
\begin{bmatrix}1&4\\3&2\end{bmatrix},
\quad
b=
\begin{bmatrix}7\\8\end{bmatrix},
\quad
c=
\begin{bmatrix}-1\\3\end{bmatrix}.
$$

这里

$$
x^TBx=-(x_1-x_2)^2,
$$

所以目标是凹函数。KKT 条件因此既必要又足够。

最优点为

$$
\boxed{
x^*=\left(\frac7{25},\frac{42}{25}\right)
=(0.28,1.68).
}
$$

第一条约束 active：

$$
\frac7{25}+4\frac{42}{25}=7,
$$

第二条有松弛：

$$
3\frac7{25}+2\frac{42}{25}=\frac{21}{5}<8.
$$

梯度

$$
c+Bx^*=
\begin{bmatrix}2/5\\8/5\end{bmatrix}
=\frac25
\begin{bmatrix}1\\4\end{bmatrix},
$$

所以可取 multiplier

$$
\lambda^*=\left(\frac25,0\right).
$$

目标值

$$
\boxed{f^*=\frac{189}{50}=3.78.}
$$

### (b)

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix},
\quad
A=
\begin{bmatrix}3&5\\1&6\end{bmatrix},
\quad
b=
\begin{bmatrix}12\\9\end{bmatrix},
\quad
c=
\begin{bmatrix}2\\2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$B\succ0$，所以目标是凸函数；在有界多面体上最大化凸函数时，至少一个最优点位于极点。枚举可行极点后最大值出现在

$$
\boxed{x^*=(4,0).}
$$

约束检查：

$$
3(4)+5(0)=12,
\qquad
4\le9.
$$

目标值

$$
2(4)+\frac12(4,0)
\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}4\\0\end{bmatrix}
=8+16=24.
$$

因此

$$
\boxed{f^*=24.}
$$

作为 KKT 验算，可取第一条约束 multiplier $\lambda_1=10/3$、第二条为 0，非负变量 $x_2=0$ 的 reduced-gradient slack 为 $56/3\ge0$。

### (c)

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
-1&1&0\\
1&-3&1\\
0&1&-1
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&-1&2\\

\end{bmatrix},
$$

$$
b=
\begin{bmatrix}1\\2\\1\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
c=
\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\\2\end{bmatrix}.
$$

$B$ 为负定矩阵，因此目标严格凹，KKT 点就是唯一全局最大点。

三条线性约束在最优点都 active，解

$$
Ax=b
$$

得到

$$
\boxed{
x^*=\left(\frac7{17},\frac2{17},\frac6{17}\right).
}
$$

其 multiplier 可由

$$
A^T\lambda=c+Bx^*

得到

$$
\boxed{
\lambda^*=\left(\frac{135}{289},\frac{60}{289},\frac{23}{289}\right)\ge0.
}
$$

目标值为

$$
\boxed{
f^*=\frac{601}{578}\approx1.03979239.
}
$$

$$
B=
\begin{bmatrix}
2&-1&0\\
-1&2&1\\

而 $A,b,c$ 与 (c) 相同。

这里 $B\succ0$，目标凸。枚举可行多面体极点可知最大值仍发生在三条约束共同交点

### 与 LCP 的联系

Theorem 8 把上述 KKT 条件统一写成

$$
u=-c-Bx+A^T\lambda\ge0,
\qquad

$$

$$
x^Tu+\lambda^Tv=0,
\qquad
x,\lambda,u,v\ge0.
$$

因此四小题也可以直接用 Chapter 4 的 complementary pivoting / LCP 框架求解。

## Exercise 8.25

若 $(x^*,\lambda^*)$ 为 saddle point，先由乘子方向的 saddle inequality 证明 $x^*$ 必可行；否则可把违反约束的乘子放大造成矛盾。再取 $\lambda=0$ 并用可行性，得 $L(x^*,\lambda^*)=f(x^*)$。对任意可行 $x$，saddle inequality 给 $f(x^*)\le f(x)$，故 $x^*$ 最优。

## Exercise 8.28

(a) 对线性规划
$$\max c^\top x\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Ax\le b,x\ge0$$
使用 $L(x,w)=c^\top x+w^\top(b-Ax)$。在线性/凸情形和严格可行假设下，$x^*$ 最优当且仅当存在 $w^*$ 使 $(x^*,w^*)$ 为 saddle pair。

(b) 若 $x^*$ 与 $w^*$ 是 primal/dual 最优解，强对偶 $c^\top x^*=b^\top w^*$，结合可行性即得 saddle inequality。

(c) 共同的 KKT/互补松弛系统为
$$A^\top w-c\ge0,\quad x\ge0,\quad x^\top(A^\top w-c)=0,$$
$$b-Ax\ge0,\quad w\ge0,\quad w^\top(b-Ax)=0.$$
因此 primal 与 dual 的 KKT 条件完全相同，只是变量解释互换。

## 本章习题复盘
核心链条：
$$\text{方向导数}\to\text{一/二阶条件}\to\text{Lagrange multiplier}\to\text{KKT}\to\text{saddle point}\to\text{QP/LCP}.$$

最常见错误不是求导，而是最大/最小化、约束方向与乘子符号不一致。正确做法是先固定 Lagrangian，再统一导出 stationarity、primal/dual feasibility 与 complementary slackness。

### Exercise 8.1At an unconstrained local minimum, $\nabla f(x^*)=0$ and $H(x^*)\succeq0$. If $\nabla f(x^*)=0$ and $H(x^*)\succ0$, Taylor's theorem gives a strict local minimum. Applying the result to $-f$ gives the maximum version.

### Exercise 8.2(a) The strict global minimum is at $(-9,-1/12,1/4)$ with value $-187/6$. (b) $(0,0)$ is a saddle and $(6,18)$ is a strict local minimum with value $-108$. (c) The local minimum is $(2,1,5)$ with value $-31/2$. (d) The unique stationary point $(-3/7,4/7,2/7)$ is a saddle.

### Exercise 8.3Use the one-dimensional restriction $\phi(t)=f(x^*+tv)$. The one-sided derivative at a constrained local maximum/minimum gives the first-order directional inequality; if the first-order term vanishes, the second derivative gives the corresponding Hessian inequality. For $x\ge0$ at a local minimum this yields $\nabla f(x^*)\ge0$ and $x^{*T}\nabla f(x^*)=0$.

### Exercise 8.4The optima are: (a) $x^*=(1,3,0)$, $f^*=2$; (b) maximum $4$ at $(0,0)$; (c) minimum at $(2,0)$ with value $16$; (d) the feasible stationary point is $x=(0,1,2\sqrt6/3)$ with the objective value shown above.

### Exercise 8.5Differentiate the identity $g(x^{(1)},h(x^{(1)}))=b$. The chain rule gives
$$
0=g_{x^{(1)}}+g_{x^{(2)}}h_{x^{(1)}},
$$
which is the textbook's Equation (10).

### Exercise 8.6Treat zero components of $x$ as active constraints and combine equality-multiplier conditions with one-sided feasible directions. This gives the nonnegativity/complementarity stationarity conditions. Every inactive inequality has zero multiplier, so the regularity assumptions need only be imposed on the active constraints for the local argument.

### Exercise 8.7Minimizing $f$ is equivalent to maximizing $-f$. Applying the equality-constrained multiplier theorem gives
$$
\nabla f(x^*)+g_x(x^*)^T\lambda^*=0,
$$
up to the equivalent sign convention for the Lagrangian.

Strict convexity makes this the unique global minimum.

### Exercise 8.10On $x_1^2+x_2^2=4$, the objective reduces to $4+x_1x_2$. Thus $f_{\max}=6$ at $(\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$ and $(-\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)$, while $f_{\min}=2$ at $(\sqrt2,-\sqrt2)$ and $(-\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$.

### Exercise 8.11Differentiate the optimal value $V(b)=f(x^*(b))$ and the constraint $g(x^*(b))=b$. Stationarity cancels the derivative of $x^*$, yielding
$$
\boxed{\partial V/\partial b_i=\lambda_i^*}.
$$
Thus a Lagrange multiplier is the marginal/shadow value of the corresponding right-hand side.

### Exercise 8.12For every tangent direction $z$ satisfying $g_x(x^*)z=0$, construct a feasible curve with derivative $z$. The second derivative of the Lagrangian along that curve gives the necessary condition
$$
\boxed{z^TH_L(x^*,\lambda^*)z\le0}
$$
for a constrained local maximum.

### Exercise 8.13If the Lagrangian Hessian is strictly negative on every nonzero feasible tangent direction, the second-order Taylor term is strictly negative on the constraint manifold, so $x^*$ is a strict local maximum. The bordered-Hessian condition is a matrix form of this tangent-space definiteness test.

### Exercise 8.14For the stated inequality problem, choose one consistent Lagrangian convention. The KKT system consists of primal feasibility, multiplier nonnegativity, complementary slackness, and the stationarity/nonnegative-variable complementarity condition displayed above. Under the stated constraint qualification these conditions are necessary.

### Exercise 8.15For mixed equality and inequality constraints, equality multipliers are unrestricted and inequality multipliers have the sign determined by the chosen convention. The complete necessary system is stationarity plus primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness for each active inequality.

### Exercise 8.16Each subproblem is obtained by specializing the same KKT template. Free variables give equality stationarity; nonnegative variables give a stationarity residual of the proper sign complementary to $x$; inequalities add nonnegative multipliers and complementary slackness. The explicit systems for (a)–(f) are written above.

### Exercise 8.17In the separating-hyperplane proof of the Kuhn–Tucker theorem, the two relevant sets are convex because $f$ is concave and the constraint functions have the required convexity. They cannot intersect without producing a feasible point with objective value strictly better than $f(x^*)$, contradicting optimality.

### Exercise 8.18Applying the separation result and normalizing the nonzero separator yields the multiplier vector and the KKT system stated in the text. The normalization is valid under the constraint qualification, which excludes a zero objective multiplier.

### Exercise 8.19For fixed $\lambda\ge0$, the Lagrangian is concave in $x$ when $f$ is concave and each $g_i$ has the required convexity; for fixed $x$ it is affine in $\lambda$. These properties yield the saddle inequalities used in the sufficiency proof.

### Exercise 8.25If $(x^*,\lambda^*)$ is a saddle point of the Lagrangian, comparing it with feasible $x$ immediately proves that $x^*$ solves the primal problem. Conversely, under convexity and a strict-feasibility (Slater-type) condition, separation gives a multiplier $\lambda^*\ge0$ such that the primal optimum and multiplier form a saddle point. In the differentiable case this is equivalent to KKT necessity and sufficiency.

### Exercise 8.26The point $x^*=(1,0)$ is the maximum of the displayed problem, but the usual KKT multipliers do not exist because the constraint qualification fails: the active constraint gradients are degenerate and no strictly feasible point exists for the squared constraint.

### Exercise 8.27Theorem 6 may impose the local regularity/convexity assumptions only on active constraints. Inactive constraints have strict slack near $x^*$ and zero multipliers, so they do not affect the local feasible cone; the required qualification must still hold for the active family.

### Exercise 8.28For the primal LP and its dual, the KKT conditions reduce exactly to primal feasibility, dual feasibility, and complementary slackness. Hence $x^*$ solves the primal iff there is a multiplier giving the saddle point in (24); if $x^*$ and $w^*$ solve the primal/dual pair, then
$$
L(x,w^*)\le L(x^*,w^*)\le L(x^*,w)
$$
with signs interpreted under the textbook convention. The KKT systems of the two LPs are identical after relabeling primal slacks and dual variables.
